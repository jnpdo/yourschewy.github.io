Images shape history.
Who holds them?

Vietnamese women have always been photographed, illustrated, and written about — in colonial newspapers, family albums, diaspora scrapbooks, girls' school records, and fashion magazines.

Most of those images have never been collected in one place. This project is trying to change that.

I'm building toward a visual and cultural history book — a collection of images, documents, and stories that traces how Vietnamese women have been seen, represented, and self-represented from the early 20th century through the diaspora.

If you have something — a photograph, a clipping, a document — I want to see it. Every contribution will be credited.

About the thesis behind this project
Harvard University · History & Literature · 2025
"Con Gái, Con Đứa!"

Controversy in the Aesthetics of Vietnam's Gái Mới (1929–1940)

In interwar Vietnam, a new figure emerged across newspapers, advertisements, and satirical cartoons: the Gái Mới — the "new girl." She wore fitted dresses. She went to school. She was modern, and she made everyone furious.

This thesis asks why the body of a young Vietnamese woman became the arena for debates about modernity, nationalism, and resistance under French colonial rule — and what her image tells us about who got to define "progress" in 1930s Vietnam.

"Why was she such a hot topic across every publication?"

— Julia Do, Harvard Hist. & Lit. Senior Spotlight, 2025

Working across women's periodicals, beauty ads, op-eds, and girls' school records, I trace how the Gái Mới was simultaneously mocked by traditionalists, celebrated by reformers, and weaponized by anti-colonial writers — all at once.

Themes
Gái Mới Colonial femininity Vietnamese press Visual culture Fashion & the body Anti-colonial resistance

The Gái Mới was not a trend. She was a political argument — her aesthetics inseparable from the shifting tides of late colonial Vietnam. This thesis is the foundation for the archive project above.
